## Investment Interpretation

The current systematic regime favors short- and intermediate-duration exposure over long Treasuries because rates have been rising over the three-month signal window.

Credit spreads, however, have tightened, so the model retains investment-grade and high-yield exposure rather than moving fully defensive.

The largest modeled tail risk is therefore not additional duration exposure but a sharp reversal in credit conditions. A 300 bp credit-spread widening produces a substantially larger estimated loss than a 100 bp parallel rate shock.

## Research Caveat

This is a systematic model view rather than a discretionary market forecast.

Credit stress uses effective duration as a first-order spread-duration proxy because historical security-level spread-duration data are not available in the public dataset. Scenario results should therefore be interpreted as sensitivity estimates rather than exact repricing forecasts.
